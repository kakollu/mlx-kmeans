Read the segments before you act on them

Segments 6 and 7 look like generous-tip and no-tip versions of the same short Manhattan trip. They are not. They are card and cash: the meter only records a tip when the fare is paid by card, so a cash trip enters the data as a 0% tipper. Those two segments are 33% of trips and are an artifact of data collection, not a difference in customers.

Worth noticing because the fix is a business decision, not a modelling one: cluster card trips only, or drop tip rate from the features and treat tipping as a separate question.

What the hardware changed

Not the method — this is ordinary k-means, the same algorithm in every stats package. What changed is that all 98.5M trips fit in memory and a pass costs milliseconds, so sampling stops being necessary.

Data: NYC TLC yellow-taxi trip records, January–August 2015 (99.8M trips; 98.5M kept after dropping implausible fares, durations and distances). Clustered on six standardised features with exact k-means — every point's assignment verified against float64.
